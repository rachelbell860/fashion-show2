## 1. The idea

- **Customer:** Who exactly is it for? Personal Fashion Show is designed for female university students aged 18–24 who enjoy fashion but have limited budgets, small closets and busy schedules. They need outfits for many occasions, including classes, work, social events and nights out.
- **Problem:** What problem do they have today, and how do they solve it now? A student might spend 20 minutes getting ready, trying on several outfits before settling on something she has already worn many times. Pinterest offers inspiration but shows clothing she does not own, and asking friends for advice is not always convenient. The result is wasted time, unnecessary purchases and underused clothing.
- **Solution:** What does your product do differently or better? Personal Fashion Show is a digital wardrobe that generates outfits from clothing users already own, based on their style preferences and which items are clean. Closet insights help users spot underused pieces and discover new combinations. Instead of encouraging customers to buy more, Personal Fashion Show helps them get more value from the wardrobes they already have.
- **Revenue:** How does the company make money? Personal Fashion Show uses a freemium model. Core wardrobe and social features are free to encourage adoption and community growth. An optional $5 monthly subscription adds convenience features for busy students: outfit calendars, weather-based recommendations and capsule wardrobe planning. The company avoids retailer advertising, because promoting new purchases would conflict with its core purpose.
